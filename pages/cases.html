The Disappearance of D.B. Cooper (1971)

what happened

On November 24, 1971, a man using the name Dan Cooper boarded a passenger airplane in the United States. During the flight, he claimed to have a bomb and demanded $200,000 and four parachutes. After receiving the money, he allowed the passengers to leave the aircraft. The plane took off again, and Cooper eventually jumped from the aircraft somewhere over the Pacific Northwest. Despite a massive investigation by the FBI, his identity and final fate were never conclusively established. Some of the ransom money was discovered years later near the Columbia River, but much of the case remains unresolved.

The Dyatlov Pass Incident (1959)

what happened

In February 1959, nine experienced hikers died during an expedition in the Ural Mountains of the Soviet Union. When search teams found their abandoned campsite, the tent had been cut open from the inside. Some of the hikers had left the tent without proper clothing in extremely cold conditions. The strange circumstances led to decades of speculation, including theories involving avalanches, military experiments and unusual weather. Later investigations suggested that a slab avalanche could explain important parts of the incident, although discussions about the exact sequence of events continue.

The Somerton Man (1948)

what happened

In December 1948, an unidentified man was discovered dead on Somerton Beach in Adelaide, Australia. He had no identification, and the labels had been removed from his clothing. Investigators found a small piece of paper in a hidden pocket containing the words "Tamám Shud," meaning "ended" or "finished" in Persian. The phrase came from a collection of poetry called the Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam. The discovery led to a complicated investigation involving a mysterious book, coded writing and attempts to identify the man. In 2022, researchers announced a DNA-based identification suggesting he was Carl Webb, an Australian electrical engineer. However, questions about the circumstances of his death remain.

The Disappearance of Amelia Earhart (1937)

what happened

Amelia Earhart was an American aviation pioneer who became famous for her achievements as a pilot. In 1937, she attempted to fly around the world with navigator Fred Noonan. During the journey, their aircraft disappeared over the Pacific Ocean near Howland Island. A major search operation followed, but neither the aircraft nor the two people aboard were found. Several theories have been suggested, including that the plane crashed into the ocean or reached another island. However, none has been definitively proven.

The Lost Colony of Roanoke (1587)

what happened

In 1587, a group of English settlers established a colony on Roanoke Island, located in present-day North Carolina. When an English supply expedition returned in 1590, the settlement appeared abandoned. The word "CROATOAN" had been carved into a wooden post, and there was no clear explanation of where the colonists had gone. Some historians believe the settlers may have moved to another location or joined nearby Indigenous communities. However, the exact fate of the entire group has never been conclusively established.
