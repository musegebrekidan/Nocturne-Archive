The bloop

what happened

In 1997, underwater listening equipment operated by the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration recorded an extremely powerful, unusual underwater sound in the South Pacific. The sound became known as "The Bloop." Because its original source was unclear, it attracted attention from scientists and the public, with some people suggesting that it could have come from an enormous unknown sea creature. However, later research connected the sound's characteristics to ice-related activity, particularly the cracking and movement of large ice formations.

The wow! signal

what happened

In August 1977, a radio telescope in Ohio detected a powerful and unusual radio signal coming from the direction of the constellation Sagittarius. The signal lasted approximately 72 seconds and appeared to have characteristics that attracted the attention of astronomers. An astronomer named Jerry Ehman later noticed the unusual reading on a computer printout and famously wrote "Wow!" beside it. That reaction gave the signal its name. Scientists have never confirmed that the signal came from extraterrestrial intelligence. Its exact origin remains uncertain, making it one of the most fascinating events in the search for life beyond Earth.

The Hessdalen Lights

what happened

In a remote valley in Norway called Hessdalen, residents have reported unusual glowing lights appearing in the sky since at least the 1930s. Reports increased significantly during the early 1980s. Some witnesses describe bright lights moving slowly across the valley, while others report objects that appear to hover before suddenly disappearing. Researchers have conducted investigations and installed equipment to observe the phenomenon. Although several possible scientific explanations have been suggested, the exact causes of all reported sightings have not been established.

The Voynich Manuscript

what happened

The Voynich Manuscript is an unusual illustrated book believed to have been created in the early 15th century. It contains strange writing that has not been reliably deciphered. Its pages include drawings of unfamiliar plants, astronomical diagrams, strange circular symbols, and illustrations of human figures. Despite decades of research by historians, linguists and codebreakers, its writing remains undeciphered. Some researchers believe it may contain an unknown language or coded information. Others have suggested different possibilities, but no explanation has been conclusively established.

The taos humm

what happened

For decades, some residents of Taos, New Mexico, have reported hearing a mysterious low-frequency humming sound. The unusual thing is that not everyone can hear it, even when standing in the same area. The sound has been described as a distant engine, a vibrating machine, or a continuous electrical noise. Researchers have investigated possible environmental and acoustic explanations, but no single source has explained every report. The phenomenon has become known as the Taos Hum and remains an interesting example of how human perception and unexplained environmental experiences can overlap.
